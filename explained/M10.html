Microbiology traps mostly hit look-alikes: organisms that share a hemolysis pattern, a colony color or a stain reaction but differ in one key test. Below, each listed trap is explained, followed by more classic micro traps and a quick way to avoid them.

Trap 1: "alpha-hemolytic strep = viridans"

S. pneumoniae is alpha-hemolytic too. Two tests separate it from viridans strep: optochin (pneumococcus is sensitive) and bile solubility (pneumococcus dissolves, thanks to its autolysin). If the optochin zone is borderline (for example after incubating in CO2), bile solubility settles it. A Gram stain with lancet-shaped diplococci and "dimpled" colonies are supporting clues.

Trap 2: Salmonella and Shigella look the same on MacConkey

Both are lactose negative (colorless). To tell them apart, use motility and H2S: Salmonella is motile and H2S positive (black on Hektoen, XLD or TSI); Shigella is non-motile and H2S negative. Remember: Salmonella "swims and stinks".

Trap 3: "acid-fast = Mycobacterium"

Acid-fast stains also pick up other organisms. Nocardia is weakly (partially) acid-fast with the modified stain. Cryptosporidium, Cyclospora and Cystoisospora oocysts in stool are acid-fast too. Always consider the specimen and the organism's size and shape: a branching rod in a lung abscess is likely Nocardia, and round 4-6 µm oocysts in stool are Cryptosporidium.

Trap 4: the thick smear names the malaria species

It doesn't. The thick smear concentrates blood to find parasites, but the red cells are lysed, so you can't judge cell size, Schüffner dots or cell shape. The thin smear keeps red cells intact and is used for species and parasite percentage.

More microbiology traps

Over-decolorized Gram stainGram-positive organisms that look pink may just be over-decolorized, old, or antibiotic-treated. Check the slide's control organisms before trusting a surprising Gram reaction.
Catalase from blood agarRed cells carried over from blood agar contain catalase and can make a streptococcus look catalase positive.
Nichrome loop for oxidaseIron-containing loops oxidise the reagent and give false positives. Use a platinum, plastic or wooden applicator.
Refrigerated CSFFastidious meningitis organisms die in the cold, giving a false-negative culture. CSF stays at room temperature and is processed at once.
Coagulase-negative staph in one bottleUsually a skin contaminant, not sepsis. Look for the same organism in more than one set before calling it significant.
Enterococcus and cephalosporinsIntrinsically resistant. Never report a cephalosporin as an option for Enterococcus.
B. anthracis vs B. cereusAnthracis is non-motile and non-hemolytic; cereus is motile and beta-hemolytic. A non-motile, non-hemolytic large Gram-positive rod means stop and refer.
Cryptococcus antigen negative but India ink full of yeastExtremely high antigen can cause a prozone (false negative). Dilute the CSF and repeat.

How to approach a microbiology trap

  1. Start from the Gram stain and the key first-split tests (catalase, oxidase, lactose, hemolysis). Most traps live at those splits.
  2. Ask what else shares that feature, and which single test separates them.
  3. Check the specimen and how it was handled: temperature, delay, container and contamination.

Check your understanding

An alpha-hemolytic coccus shows a small optochin zone after CO2 incubation. Next step?

Bile solubility test. If the colony dissolves, it is S. pneumoniae.

A colorless MacConkey colony from stool is motile and turns TSI black. Salmonella or Shigella?

Salmonella (motile, H2S positive).

A modified acid-fast stain of a lung abscess shows thin branching rods that are partly red. Likely organism?

Nocardia.
