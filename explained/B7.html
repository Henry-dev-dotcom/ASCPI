Blood bank traps usually test whether you know an antibody's class (IgM or IgG), how it reacts to chemical treatments, and whether a test is valid. Each listed trap is explained below, followed by more classics.

Trap 1: "every antibody can cause HDFN"

Only IgG crosses the placenta, so only IgG antibodies can coat fetal red cells. Lewis and P1 antibodies are IgM: they can't cross. Lewis antigens are also poorly developed on fetal cells. So anti-Lewis and anti-P1 don't cause HDFN. Rh antibodies (anti-D, anti-c, anti-E) are IgG and do. So do Kell, Duffy and Kidd.

Trap 2: "enzymes enhance everything"

Ficin and papain strip proteins and sugars from the red cell surface. That enhances antigens that were partly hidden (Rh, Kidd, Lewis, I, P1) but destroys antigens that sit on the stripped-away parts (Duffy, M, N, S). So a panel run with enzymes makes anti-Fya disappear while anti-E gets stronger. This is a useful way to split mixtures of antibodies.

Trap 3: "a negative AHG test is negative"

Only if the check cells work. IgG-coated check cells are added to every negative AHG tube; if they don't clump, the AHG was neutralised (usually by poor washing) or never added, and the "negative" result is invalid. Repeat the test.

Trap 4: universal donors

The two "universal" products are opposites, and the exam swaps them on purpose:

  • Red cells: group O is universal (no A or B antigen on the cells).
  • Plasma: group AB is universal (no anti-A or anti-B in the plasma).

For emergency red cells, give O D-negative to girls and women who may become pregnant; O D-positive is often used for men and older women when O-negative stock is low.

How to approach a blood bank trap

  1. Is the antibody IgM or IgG? That predicts the phase it reacts in, whether it matters clinically, and whether it crosses the placenta.
  2. What do enzymes, DTT, temperature and dosage do to it?
  3. Is the test valid? Check cells, controls, sample age and identification come first.

Check your understanding

A pregnant woman has anti-Lea. Is her baby at risk of HDFN?

No. Anti-Lewis is IgM (doesn't cross the placenta) and Lewis antigens are poorly expressed on fetal cells.

An antibody reacts with untreated panel cells but not with ficin-treated cells. Which specificities fit?

Duffy (anti-Fya, anti-Fyb), anti-M, anti-N (and often anti-S).

Which blood group is the universal donor for plasma?

Group AB.
