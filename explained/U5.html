Urinalysis and body fluid traps nearly always involve a test that misses something (a false negative) or two things that look alike under the microscope. Each listed trap is explained below, followed by more classics.

Trap 1: "a negative nitrite rules out UTI"

The nitrite test only works if three things are true: the bacteria can convert nitrate to nitrite (mostly Gram-negative Enterobacterales), the urine sat in the bladder long enough (about 4 hours), and there was nitrate from the diet to convert. So nitrite is falsely negative with Enterococcus, staph and other Gram-positives, with frequent urination, with a low-vegetable diet, and with vitamin C (which interferes with the reaction). Use leukocyte esterase, microscopy and culture too.

Trap 2: "a negative dipstick protein means no protein"

The protein pad reacts mainly with albumin. It misses Bence Jones protein (free light chains) and most globulins. If myeloma is suspected, use sulfosalicylic acid (SSA), which precipitates all proteins, and specific tests (immunofixation, free light chains). The opposite trap: a very alkaline urine can make the pad falsely positive.

Trap 3: blood in CSF always means hemorrhage

Blood from a traumatic tap (the needle nicked a vessel) clears from tube 1 to tube 3, and the supernatant is clear. A true subarachnoid hemorrhage shows the same amount of blood in every tube and a yellow (xanthochromic) supernatant from broken-down red cells.

Trap 4: yeast counted as red cells

Yeast and red cells are a similar size and both look round and pale. Clues: yeast buds, has no central pallor, and may form pseudohyphae; red cells don't. Add dilute acetic acid: red cells lyse, yeast doesn't. Oil droplets are another mimic: they vary in size and are very refractile.

More urinalysis and fluid traps

Old urineA sample left out for hours shows high pH, high bacteria, low glucose and ketones, low bilirubin, and dissolved casts. Don't report it as if fresh.
Vitamin CIt falsely lowers the glucose, blood, bilirubin, nitrite and leukocyte esterase pads.
Dipstick ketones in DKAThe pad misses beta-hydroxybutyrate, the main ketone in severe DKA.
Refractometer and contrast dyeGlucose, protein and X-ray contrast raise refractometer SG. A value above 1.040 suggests contrast dye, not real concentration.
Hemoglobin vs myoglobinBoth give a positive blood pad without red cells. Check the plasma color (pink = hemoglobin) and the CK (very high = myoglobin).
Synovial fluid diluentAcetic acid forms a mucin clot with synovial fluid. Use saline (or hyaluronidase) for cell counts.
Crystal birefringenceGout crystals are yellow when parallel (strong negative); pseudogout crystals are blue when parallel (weak positive). Swapping the colors is a classic error.
Refrigerated CSF for cultureNever refrigerate CSF for bacterial culture; fastidious organisms die.

How to approach a urinalysis trap

  1. Check the specimen first: age, storage, collection type and contamination (squamous cells).
  2. Ask what each pad or test actually detects, and what it misses.
  3. Confirm with a second method: microscopy, SSA, Clinitest, culture or plasma color.

Check your understanding

Leukocyte esterase positive, nitrite negative, many white cells, culture grows Enterococcus. Why was nitrite negative?

Enterococcus doesn't reduce nitrate to nitrite.

Round bodies in urine sediment don't dissolve with acetic acid and some are budding. What are they?

Yeast.

A urine SG reads 1.050 on the refractometer after a CT scan. Why?

X-ray contrast dye in the urine raises the refractometer reading. The dipstick or osmolality gives a truer value.
