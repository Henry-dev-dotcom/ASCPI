Enzymes are proteins that live inside cells. When cells are damaged, enzymes leak into the blood, and because different organs contain different enzymes, the pattern of what leaks tells you which organ is hurt. Cardiac markers work the same way, with one more element: timing, because each marker rises and falls on its own schedule after a heart attack.

Cardiac markers: think about timing

After a heart attack (myocardial infarction, MI), heart cells die and release their contents. Small molecules escape first; bigger or more tightly bound ones take longer and linger.

  • Myoglobin: a small oxygen-holding protein. It rises first (1-3 h), peaks at 6-9 h and is gone within about 24 h. But skeletal muscle has it too, so it is not specific. Its main use is that a normal myoglobin a few hours after pain makes MI unlikely.
  • CK-MB: the heart form of creatine kinase. Rises at 3-6 h, peaks 12-24 h, normal again in 2-3 days. Because it clears quickly, it is useful for spotting a second (re-)infarction.
  • Troponin I or T: part of the heart's contraction machinery. The cardiac forms exist only in heart muscle, so troponin is the most specific and the preferred marker. Rises at 3-4 h (sooner with high-sensitivity assays), peaks 12-24 h, and stays high for 7-10 days or more. That makes it the best marker for a patient who arrives days after chest pain.
  • BNP / NT-proBNP: not a damage marker. The stretched ventricle releases it, so it helps diagnose heart failure.
Memory hook: first in, first outMyoglobin is first in and first out (1 day). CK-MB stays a couple of days. Troponin arrives a little later but stays over a week, so it is the "The long Timer".

Liver enzymes: two kinds of injury

Liver tests split into two groups, and which group is high tells you the type of problem:

Hepatocellular (liver cell) injury → AST and ALT. These enzymes are inside liver cells, so they spill out when cells are inflamed or die (viral hepatitis, drugs, toxins).

  • ALT is mostly in the liver, so it is more liver-specific.
  • AST is also in heart, muscle and red cells.
  • In viral hepatitis, ALT is usually ≥ AST. An AST:ALT ratio above 2, especially with high GGT, suggests alcoholic liver disease (alcohol damages mitochondria, where much of the AST lives, and lowers the vitamin B6 that ALT needs).

Cholestatic (bile flow) injury → ALP and GGT. These enzymes line the bile ducts and rise when bile can't flow (gallstones, tumors, drug cholestasis).

  • ALP is also made by bone-building cells (osteoblasts), so it rises in bone disease, growing children and pregnancy (placental ALP).
  • To tell whether a high ALP comes from liver or bone, check GGT or 5'-nucleotidase: high with ALP → liver; normal → bone.
  • GGT also rises with alcohol use and some drugs.

Pancreatic enzymes

In acute pancreatitis, digestive enzymes leak into the blood. Amylase rises within hours but falls back in 2-3 days, and the salivary glands also make it (mumps, salivary disease). Lipase is more specific for the pancreas and stays high longer (up to 1-2 weeks), so it is the preferred test.

CK and its three isoenzymes

Creatine kinase is made of two subunits, M (muscle) and B (brain). The three combinations point to different tissues:

  • CK-MM: skeletal muscle (most of the CK in blood). Very high after crush injury, rhabdomyolysis, intense exercise or IM injections.
  • CK-MB: heart.
  • CK-BB: brain, rarely seen in blood.

LD and the "flipped" pattern

Lactate dehydrogenase is in nearly every cell, so total LD is not specific. Its five isoenzymes help. Normally LD2 is higher than LD1. Heart muscle and red cells are rich in LD1, so in MI, hemolysis and megaloblastic anemia LD1 rises above LD2: the "flipped" pattern. LD is also high in many cancers (cell turnover).

Other enzymes to recognise

  • Acid phosphatase: once used for prostate cancer, now replaced by PSA. Still used in forensic testing for semen.
  • Cholinesterase (pseudocholinesterase): low in organophosphate insecticide poisoning; also tested before using the anesthetic succinylcholine, because low levels cause prolonged paralysis.

How enzyme activity is measured

Enzymes are measured by what they do, not by how much protein there is. Many reactions are linked to the coenzyme NAD/NADH. NADH absorbs light strongly at 340 nm; NAD does not. So the analyzer watches absorbance at 340 nm rise or fall over time. The rate of change gives the enzyme activity (a kinetic assay).

Why temperature and timing matter for enzymesEnzymes are sensitive to temperature, pH and substrate. Reactions must be read while the rate is steady (the linear phase), with plenty of substrate. If the enzyme activity is extremely high, the substrate gets used up too fast ("substrate depletion") and the result reads falsely low, so dilute and rerun.

Check your understanding

A patient comes in 6 days after chest pain. Which marker is most likely still raised?

Troponin. It stays up 7-10 days or more; CK-MB and myoglobin are back to normal.

ALP is high but GGT is normal. Where is the ALP coming from?

Most likely bone (or placenta in pregnancy). A liver source would raise GGT too.

Why is lipase preferred over amylase for pancreatitis?

It is more specific to the pancreas and stays raised longer.

AST 220, ALT 90, GGT high. Most likely cause?

Alcoholic liver disease (AST:ALT above 2 with raised GGT).
